UT2003/4

Team Invasion

Invasion played with two teams. The version that I released to the public had a fatal flaw in which a configuarble option was never probably set to a default (in this case the Player Health, would be set to 0 by default.). This would cause the game to crash. This is a problem, since most people don't take the time to look the options over before playing the game. I never did get around to releasing the fixed version (along with several other bug fixes.)

MythServer

An all-in-one custom mutator I created for my server. It has evolved a lot over the time that I have worked on it. The current version has a couple of options for 'glowing' and what is unique about it is that it uses what players say to fire off different commands. IE. if the player said 'rules' the server would 'say' the rules to all the players. If the admin said 'slay all' the server would slay everyone (with the exception of that specific admin). Very cool I think.

MythQuest

Gotta love that name. It is the working title of a new, somewhat total conversion for UT2004. I don't want to start out with saying it will be the greatest thing ever, so... I'm not heh. It will be an RPG (creatively inspired by me recently playing Divine Divinity (not a bad game once you get used to it all. Lots and Lots of bugs to fix... and it's been ot for 5 years... it's a wonder why not all of them have been squashed :| ). Gameplay hasn't been thought out that much as I am still in pre-production stages. It will either be, internet-server based (not exactly an MMORPG, but close I guess), or single-player based. Either way, I'm sure of this much. Each player will have a certain amount of experience needed to gain levels (as in almost ALL RPG's). Players gain XP by either Killing enemies, completing quests, or succesfully creating items (such as armor, weapons, cooking etc.). This is about where it could go either way, at the moment. I want to have different 'skills' and abilities for the player to use. With every new level the player will gain a few skillpoints. He can then choose what skills/abilities he wants to give skillpoints to. The higher the level you are in each skill, the better you do in that particular skill. For example, if you have a low cooking level, you might only be able to cook, say fish. If you had a high cooking level, you could be able to cook pizza. There's another way I think I prefer to do it though. This way will be much like Runescape's way (not sure if many of you have played it. I think it's great though.) in which each skill will have it's own seperate experience point counters. You gain experience in each skill by doing what that skill demands. For example, if you cook a fish, you could say 400 cooking experience. If you chopped a tree down, you would get 600 woodcutting experience. And once you reach the required amount of experience, you gain a level in that particular skill. In this game, you will be able to do a lot of stuff. Cook, Fish, Make Armor/Weapons, just be a miner. Or perhaps, you could be a Hermit Of Knowledge... and sit in a cave in the mountains and learn all sorts of interesting things... I have a few ideas for 'special features' too, although thinking that far ahead will probably kill all of this sooner than later. Anyways, here's one. Once a players smithing level (the ability to make weapons/armor whatever) reaches a certain level, they will be able to make customized weapons/armor complete with names and other special abilities (rather than having to make pre-determined armor... such as Bronze Axe, or Steel Sword). There is much debate amonst the little voices in my head as to whether or not I should do a class-based limiting system, in which if you are a 'Warrior' you can only go to a certain amount of levels in the Magic skill, and if you are a 'Magician' you can learn how to use weapons, or you can't use them as well. As progress goes, I will most likely be documenting some of it on the wiki to help others.
