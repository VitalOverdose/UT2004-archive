Mac

This page is a small 'guide' for Mac users who may want educating on the subject of the Mac versions of Unreal Engine games.

Unreal

Ported by [Westlake Interactive] and published by [Macsoft], but never really finished in terms of patches, which makes network games rather incompatible with the Windows version. It also has a nasty bug that occurs on some graphics cards that causes lighting to be extremely dark and not appear well at all making it very difficult to see where the hell you're going on the darker maps.

Patches

Unfortunately, there are only 2 known patches to the game, and the last released patch was only a beta and requires the previous one to be installed. Both are becoming extremely hard to come by, although sooner or later I'll probably stick them online for people to download. They fix a *huge* amount of bugs too, so it's generally recommended one finds, downloads and installs them.

Return to Na Pali

The Digital Extremes 'expansion pack' for Unreal. This was never released on the Mac, and unfortunately, it's not actually a 'drop-in' modification (doesn't require the original Unreal to already be installed), and can't run at all on a Mac for several reasons, but mainly because of the fact it has several system files that have Windows-only content in them, and thus can't work on a Mac.

Editing: Maps & Mods

Impossible I'm afriad (I go into more detail about the same problem with Unreal Tournament below). It was obvious from the outset no Mac version of UnrealED would be released - Which was a major shame, but, now all in the past.

Unreal Tournament

It has had its fair share of problems (probably more so than the Windows version). After the initial release of UT for Windows, it was decided to get some third party porters in to do the Mac version (which at the time was quite understandable, since the Mac has never been recognised as a great gaming platform, which indeed up until recently, was a well founded fact).

The job of porting it was given to Westlake Interactive yet again. The Publisher would also be Macsoft yet again, although at a later stage Infograms (AKA Atari), took over support for the Mac version, even though they had nothing to do with it.

Patches

The last official patch made available (in sync with the Windows version of the game) was version 436 (which should be downloadable from [Atari's Support Site]). It squished numerous bugs, and even brought in some new ones (as most patches often do). However, there still remains the most infamous bug of them all:

The Sound Bug

This extremely annoying bug I refer to as The Sound Bug usually occurs on most Mac systems. Although it's still a bit weird. You might be able to play the game perfectly for a short period of time, without experiencing this problem. But it may become apparent after a few Map changes that music no longer plays, sound effects don't seem to play at all, or if they do, the wrong sound plays and usually gets echoed to death. There is no known fix for this problem, despite what Westlake have said about suggesting you use different versions of the Sound Manager. Unfortunately, they've now dropped support for the game altogether.

Westlake even reported they were working on a fix, but they couldn't reproduce the bug on any system they tried. God knows what systems they were using as I've managed to reproduce on every Mac I've played the game on (probably about 5 or so of varying setups). It is true that some people do not appear to suffer from the problem on their Mac (lucky them), although how and why is beyond mine and many others' comprehension. It's quite likely the Sound Card (which these days is nearly always integrated into the logicboard AKA motherboard) that causes the problem. But the fact remains Westlake never fixed it, and never will. Go them. Gits... They weren't exactly in my good books to start with, but that's a rant for another day.

There is some hope though, mainly for Mac OS X users. As Epic's Ryan Gordon, is *attempting* to make a Mac10 version of the game. I've no idea if he'll be incorperating this into a [UTPG] patch or if the game will remain at 436. Either way, there's no set release date as of yet. And I'm sceptical as to whether or not anything will come of it anyway but anyway, keep an eye on [this page] just in case.

Editing: Maps & Mods

Unfortunately, the same problem that fell upon the original Unreal Mac version falls upon this game. It is not possible to make Maps or Mods if you have the Mac version of the game. That's because there is no UnrealEd for Mac, as nearly all Mac users who play UT are probably well aware of. There is also no UnrealScript compiler available, or indeed other less important tools that might have been useful such as a UMOD creation tool (even though Westlake had the kindness in their heart to create a tool to install UMOD files which comes with the 436 patch). Don't cry though, as there is a project called Seppuku (which literally translates as something like Suicidally Ambitious).

It's highly unlikely the one guy (Paul Snively) will ever succeed in finishing the project. More disappointing was his decision to make it cross platform, which to me seems a bit of a risk-take considering it will double his work load (unless he gets more people on the project). Although he seems to think it'll make his life easier in the long-run. Anyway, drop some lines of support if you feel the need too. He'd appreciate it, and it would probably give him a bigger incentive to carry on with his work.

As far as I know there are no other projects around that plan to try and make an Editor for UT, and don't even attempt to expect a port of UnrealEd, that would be too hard to do, and would probably cost a much to port as the game did. Anyway, UT's wholly unsupported all round now so, even if a UED port might have been on the cards way back when (which it wasn't), it certainly won't be now.

Emulators & UT for Windows

I have, believe it or not, used UnrealED on my Macintosh. How I hear you cry? With a dodgy emulator of course! I happened to aquire a piece of software called Virtual PC (once developed by Connectix but now owend by Microsoft). VPC is a x86 emulator for the Mac. And after tinkering with it, you can then proceed to run a copy of Windows on it... do you see where I'm going with this? Indeed. All you have to do upon completion of that task, is obtain yourself a copy of UT for Windows, install it and get started using UnrealED. But it's not only a slow venture, there are a few other problems you might want to know about, namely the fact emulators eat resources - VPC is no exception. I do not recomend you use this option. Believe me, I've tried it, and it was painfully slow.

I'm only mentioning this to inform, I'm certainly not actually suggesting you go out and get VPC for the only reason that there's no UED for Mac! Oh, and before you go crapping yourself with excitment thinking you'll actually be able to play UT for Windows (so as to avoid The Sound Bug) as well as various other PC games never released for the Mac, think again. There is no Graphics Card support in version 4 and above of VPC (this will likely never change). What that basically means is you can only play games that run software rendered. And even though UT can be software rendered, we're talking about 2 Frames per second here. Of course if you've got some kind of mega Mac like a super duper über over-powered, over-priced G5, then it might be (well, more than likely will be) much faster. But still not PC quality. Really, if you're desperate to make maps or mods, get a real PC.

And if you do decide to get VPC anyway, I suggest you don't buy Version 6 or above... Those versions were made by Microsoft, and I've heard the quality of the program went down hill when they started developing it. No surprise there then...

UT 2003 & 2004

Yes, both were released for the Mac. As it happens, this time around Epic took on the job or creating Mac versions (yay), probably because Westlake pretty much buggered the last two games up. So I suspect there won't be any repeat performances of nasty unfixed bugs. Although still no UnrealED as of yet (bummer). I don't actually have either of these games, mainly because I "stayed faithful" to UT... and more to the point I can't play either games because they're OS X only and I've got OS 9 still... not to mention the fact my computer's got a crap graphics card. So I can't really given any more info on them than that.

Other Games

There have been numerous other non-Unreal games released on the Mac that use the Unreal Engine. Details of exactly what games have been released can be found on the Unreal Engine Versions page, although as for which got Mac releases, some of the more popular titles include Deus Ex, Rune, Undying and even the newer XIII. None, if I recall correctly, have a Mac version of the map editor available... there's a surprise.

Postal 2

If you're a sadistic Mac user, you'll be pleased to know Postal 2 is being ported to the Mac by none other than Ryan Gordon (he deserves a medal). P2 was essentially just a total conversion of UT2K3 anyway, so I wouldn't have thought it would have been that much trouble to port (Shame on you, Running with Scissors). Once again, you can keep an eye on the project as well as other things on his update page located [here].
