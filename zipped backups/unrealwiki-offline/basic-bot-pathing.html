Basic Bot Pathing

This page is obsolete. See Navigation AI.

Material on this page needs to be merged where appropriate.

Pathnoding is considered by most mappers to be the "dirty work" of mapping. It can be difficult, frustrating and seem totally unnecessary all at the same time, especially if you prefer to play over a LAN or online. All the same, it is a necessary step, and the better the pathnoding, the better the reviews your map will receive.

Bots navigate a map by moving through a network of NavigationPoints, the simplest of which is called a PathNode. When a bot wants to get somewhere, the game finds the node nearest his current position and the node nearest his objective, calculates the shortest path through the network from one to the other, and tells the bot to follow that path. Thus in order to get bots to play your map, you need to place enough PathNodes for bots to be able to use them to get to anywhere they might need to go.

Bring up the Actor Browser, and under NavigationPoint, select PathNode to make it your current actor. Now go through your level in 3D view and place nodes everywhere you want your bots to be able to go by right-clicking on the floor and selecting Add PathNode. For a bot to be able to move from one node to another, the two nodes must be within sight of one another, and not too far apart. A distance of 300 to 700 UUs seems to work well. Place them closer together on steep ramps or staircases, and farther apart on open floors. Be careful not to place too many; an excessive number of nodes may slow your map down, and is likely to confuse bots. Note that other types of NavigationPoints such as PlayerStarts, FlagBases in CTF maps, ControlPoints in Domination maps, and InventorySpots (which are created automatically whenever you place an item that can be picked up) also act as nodes in the bot pathing network, so you don't need to add PathNodes near those items.

To see the path network you're building, right-click on the grey bar at the top of the 3D viewport to bring up the viewport context menu and select "Show Paths" under the view submenu. You'll need to rebuild your paths (use the Build Paths button on the toolbar) for any changes you make to take effect. Also, do not use PathNodes to try to make bots use lifts or jump off edges; there are different tools for that, and you'll get to them later. PathNodes generally only suffice for basic movement across floors, ramps, and staircases; although sometimes, bots will figure out how to use triggers without any extra help from you. The damage-triggered door in DM-Curse][ for the original Unreal Tournament is a good example. There isn't any special pathing required to get the bots to use it.

A nice alternative to doing this manually in UnrealEd is to use PathLogic, a convenient program which will allow you to place nodes in your map while playing it in the game itself. This may not be necessary for small maps, but for larger endeavors it can be a real time-saver.

Whichever method you choose, once you've got the whole level covered with PathNodes, it's time to handle more advanced behaviors such as riding lifts, using jump pads, hammer jumps, and translocators. Some pages to check out when you're ready to proceed are:

  • Pathing Lifts
  • Pathing Kickers
  • Pathing Translocators

Some minor notes:

  • Bots like inventory items – use them as incentive to make a bot follow a route. Remember that inventory items also act as pathnodes, whether they are spawned or not, so there's no need to put a pathnode at the item.
  • Occasionally a bot gets 'stuck' and keeps running from one node to another, back and forth. The are two possible causes for this:
    • There's no link to another pathnode on the other side of a 'dead end' in pathing. The bot keeps trying to get somewhere but can't. Solution: make another node to allow the bot to reach the other side of the dead end.
    • The bot is trying to go to a pathnode, inventory item or LiftExit/Center and cannot reach it. Solution: Move things around a bit, make them a little farther/closer to the bot. Keep experiementing until the problem ceases.
  • If you ever select Add PathNode and no node appears, it means that there wasn't room to add it. Keep trying in different spots nearby until it works.
  • Don't forget that too many pathnodes can slow the map down; the more nodes in the network, the more time it takes to calculate a path through it. So don't go overboard.
