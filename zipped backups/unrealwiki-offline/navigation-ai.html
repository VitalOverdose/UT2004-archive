Navigation AI

This is the main topic page for computer-controlled things that are able to move around maps: bots, monsters, and so on.

Overview

One of the reasons for the success of the Unreal engine games is the realistic "AI": bots that kick your butt and by and large appear to play as if they were human. This is accomplished with two elements working together:

  1. The map contains a navigation network, or "system of waypoints" in the words of Epic programmer Steve Polge. This is commonly called the Botpath network, and it is up to the mapper to design this network by placing special actors in the map. This reduces the wonderfully detailed environment that the human player sees to something not much more complex than a level of Pacman: the bots just follow the dots.
  2. ...and they do this with the UnrealScript code in the Bot class, which interacts with the code in the navigation point classes. On a low level, bots are able to walk, run, and jump to get to a specific point of the map. Beyond this, they need the navigation network to tell them where they can get to and why they might want to go there.

Building the network

The average mapper need only concern himself with the first aspect of navigation AI: the creation of a botpath network in the map. This topic area is split into several pages:

  • Bot Navigation – an overview of the general principles of bots.
  • Bot Pathing – instructions for creating a basic bot path network
